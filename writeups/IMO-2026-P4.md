**Initial Observations**

For Mulan to force a win, she must eventually cut $\mathcal{T}$ into two triangles,
each containing at least one angle measuring $\theta$.
Otherwise, Shan-Yu could avoid her immediate victory by picking a triangle that does not.
Let us assume that this cut looks like the picture.

Note that the two angles $\beta$ and $\gamma$ cannot be equal to $\theta$,
since otherwise Mulan would have already won. Thus, the only two options are
$$\angle APB = \angle CPA \quad\text{or}\quad \angle BAP = \angle PAC.$$
The first option implies $\theta = 90^\circ$, the second implies that $\alpha = 2\theta$.

If $\theta = 90^\circ$, then Mulan can indeed win by making a single cut along one of the altitudes of $\mathval{T}$,
forcing Shan-Yu to pick among two right-angled triangles.
Similarly, if $\mathcal{T}$ contains an angle measuring $2\theta$,
then Mulan can cut along the corresponding angle bisector to guarantee her victory in the next round.

A similar argument shows that Mulan wins if $\mathcal{T}$ has an angle measuring $3\theta$,
since she can split it into $2\theta$ and $\theta$, both leading to victories.
By induction, we conclude that if $\mathcal{T}$ has an angle that measures a multiple of $\theta$, Mulan can win.

The observations above suggest that this can only be done around the point $P$, since this is where Mulan can
